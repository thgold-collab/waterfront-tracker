The one number that runs the day

It is 1 hour 48 minutes home from Kilmarnock. Everything else is negotiable; that is not. Leave Kilmarnock by two o'clock and you are home by a quarter to four with fifteen minutes in hand. Leave at half past two and you are late.

That single fact is why this is the shorter loop. Out on US‑33 through Middlesex, over the Norris Bridge, back down US‑360 — still a loop, still two river crossings, and Saluda, Kilmarnock, Warsaw and Tappahannock still come free because the road goes through all four.

  1. 7:15

    Depart Glen Allen — I‑64 East, then US‑33 East through West Point

  2. 8:30 drive-by

    Saluda

    Middlesex County seat · five minutes on US‑33

    The road goes through it. You pass the courthouse green, which tells you what the administrative side of county life looks like, and you keep going — coffee is fifteen minutes further on.

  3. 8:40

    Urbanna — and coffee

    Middlesex County · a colonial tobacco port since the 1680s · allow ninety minutes

    One of Virginia's original official ports, still small enough to walk end to end. Coffee first, then Cross Street down to the harbour on Urbanna Creek, past the 1766 tobacco warehouse.

    The Wooden Pickle — coffee, baked goods, sandwiches
    Saturday 6:00 am – 8:00 pm
    Open from six, so it does not matter how early you leave. Note it is closed Wednesdays and Thursdays — Saturday is one of its good days, which is luck rather than planning. It appears to have taken over 51 Cross Street, the premises of the Cross Street Coffee & Cafe that closed, though one listing still gives its address as 280 Virginia St. Both are the same two blocks; the map pin below searches by name so it will find it either way.
    Hours from a Yelp listing updated May 2026 and the cafe's own Facebook page, not from a call.
    If it has changed hands again: Big Oak Cafe (Sat 7:30 am – 1:30 pm) and Virginia Street Cafe (Sat 8 am – 8 pm) are both in town.
    The Wooden Pickle →

    This is the town on your list that most rewards being judged on foot rather than on a map. Whether you would happily spend a Saturday here is a question only the sidewalk answers — and it is the useful contrast with Irvington later, which is prettier but less of a working place.

  4. 10:35

    The Norris Bridge — and White Stone

    VA‑3 · nearly two miles across

    The crossing is the event. You come up over the span and the Rappahannock opens out underneath you close to its widest — the best single look at the river you will get, and the moment that explains why people move out here. White Stone sits at the far end: a crossroads village, and twenty minutes is honestly the right amount of time for it.

    The timing here is deliberate. Lunch does not open until eleven, so the bridge and the village are what you do with the twenty-five minutes rather than sitting in a car park.

  5. 11:00

    Lunch on the Rappahannock

    White Stone · 327 Old Ferry Rd · an hour

    Better than anything I could confirm in Irvington, and it is right where you come off the bridge rather than a detour. On the river, casual, and no oysters required.

    Willaby's on the Rappahannock
    Saturday 11:00 am – 3:00 pm · (804) 435‑0000
    First come, first served — they do not take reservations, which is the argument for arriving as they open rather than at half past twelve. Arriving at eleven sharp is also what keeps the four o'clock honest.
    Hours from Yelp (April 2026) and Sirved; the restaurant's own site was not reachable from here. Worth one call.
    Backup, ten minutes on: Golden Eagle Grill at the golf club in Irvington, listed Wed–Mon 11 am – 3 pm, (804) 438‑4460. Trick Dog is not an option — it is dinner only from 5 pm, and one source lists it as closed for good.

  6. 12:15

    Irvington

    Lancaster County · Carter's Creek · thirty-five minutes

    The prettiest village on the Neck, and eight minutes from lunch. Walk the lanes and the Tides Inn grounds down to Carter's Creek. If Urbanna is the working port, this is the postcard, and the contrast between the two is the most useful thing the day gives you.

  8. 1:55 drive-by

    Kilmarnock

    Main Street · on the way to US‑360 · costs three minutes

    The practical town of the group — the one with the shops you would genuinely need, and the answer to "but where would we actually buy anything". Routing through it rather than around it costs three minutes, so take Main Street slowly and keep moving. This is your two o'clock checkpoint.

  9. 2:40 drive-by

    Warsaw

    Richmond County seat · US‑360 runs through it

    Another small county seat, and a useful third data point on what these towns are like when nobody is selling you anything. Seen through a windscreen at forty-five minutes from Kilmarnock.

  10. 2:50 drive-by

    Tappahannock

    Essex County · where US‑360 crosses the Rappahannock

    Your second river crossing, forty miles upstream from the first — a good way to end, because the contrast between the Rappahannock here and at the Norris Bridge is the whole geography of the day in two views.

    One of these two, not both. Actually pulling into both town centres costs about fifteen minutes over staying on the road, which is precisely the slack in the day. Pick whichever you fancy when you get there, or keep driving and bank the quarter of an hour.

  11. ~3:45

    Home on US‑360 West, with fifteen minutes spare

What the four o'clock costs you

Reedville — and the one thing Saturday would have given you
The Reedville end of the Neck holds 2082 Fairport Rd ($539,000, score 61): 1951, but roof, HVAC, windows and siding all replaced within four years, a private pier and a boat lift, no HOA, and a fresh cut from $549,900. Of everything on the board it has the fewest unknowns. Main Street up there is menhaden-money Victorians, and the Fishermen's Museum at 504 Main is open 11–4 on Saturdays — the exact thing that was shut when this was a Sunday plan.

And it does not fit. Reedville is 2 hours 4 minutes out and the museum does not open until eleven, so going there first means waiting an hour on the doorstep, and going there last means getting home at half past four. The four o'clock is what rules it out, not the day of the week.

On its own it is an easy Saturday: leave at eight, museum at eleven, the house at half past twelve, home by three. That is the button above.

Before you go

  • Leaving earlier buys morning, not margin. The four o'clock is fixed by the drive home, so an earlier start lengthens Urbanna rather than adding anything at the far end. Leave at 6:45 and you get half an hour more on the water at Urbanna; the rest of the card stays where it is, because lunch cannot start before eleven.
  • The house is a drive‑by unless you call. Saturday showings need an appointment, and the call is also how you get the depth figure — so it is worth making either way.
  • Opening hours here are from listing aggregators, not from calls. Two sites I wanted for the museum and the restaurant were blocked from where I checked. The Wooden Pickle, Willaby's and the museum all have phone numbers on this card; one call each before Saturday and the day is solid.
  • Drive times are real — routed door to door, not estimated. They carry no allowance for traffic, roadworks or a slow left turn onto US‑360, which is where the fifteen minutes of slack goes.
  • Check the first pin before you set off. Every address was run through a geocoder. Willaby's and the Reedville house match exactly; E Fairway Dr matches to the street but not the house number, so glance at that one when Maps opens it.

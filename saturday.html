What taking Reedville out actually did

It swapped one long spur for another, in the opposite direction. Montross is two hours from home and seventy-six minutes past Kilmarnock, so the day is not shorter — it is 6h03 against 5h35. What it buys is somewhere to be at the far end: a state park with fossil cliffs on the Potomac, and Stratford Hall five minutes further on.

One thing the map hides. Montross's houses sit on separate necks, and Matthews Cove to Edgewater Lane is fifty-two minutes despite being four miles apart, because you drive back inland around Nomini Creek to get between them. That is why only one Montross house is on this card and the other two are listed underneath instead.

  6. 12:45 walk it

    Montross

    Westmoreland County seat · fifteen minutes

    Small county seat, courthouse, a few blocks. Fifteen minutes is its measure and it is on the way to the park. The useful question is not whether Montross charms you but whether it has what you would need, because at two hours out it is the furthest thing on your list from home.

  7. 1:10 the reason to come

    Westmoreland State Park — lunch on the fossil beach

    On the Potomac · $7 to park · open dawn to dusk · fifty minutes

    This is the answer to Reedville having nothing to do. Horsehead Cliffs rise straight out of the Potomac here, and they are Miocene marine sediment that erodes continuously, dropping shells, shark teeth and the occasional whale bone onto the beach below, where the waves clean and concentrate them. People come specifically to pick them up.

    Open dawn to dusk every day, so no hours to work around. Eat what you brought here.

    One honest catch on the fossils
    Saturday — dawn to dusk, $7 parking
    Fossil Beach is 0.6 miles each way on the Big Meadow Trail from the visitor-centre car park, and the return leg is uphill, because it is a cliff. That is a 1.2‑mile round trip on top of lunch. Fifty minutes covers lunch and the overlook comfortably; the full walk down needs about thirty minutes more than this card allows, and the slack in the day is twenty. Decide when you get there — the cliffs are the view either way.
    Hours and fee from Virginia DCR's park page; not re-checked by phone.
    Stratford Hall is five minutes further — the 1730s great house on the Potomac where Robert E. Lee was born. A proper visit needs an hour or more, which this day does not have, but it is the obvious anchor if you ever come up here for a whole weekend.

If Montross is what you actually want, give it its own Saturday
Driven direct it is two hours each way, and a day that starts at Montross instead of ending there fits all three houses, both of the far-apart ones, Westmoreland State Park and Stratford Hall without the six hours of driving. Today's version pays 76 minutes to reach Westmoreland from Kilmarnock; a dedicated day spends that on the ground instead.

Bay Creek, at Cape Charles

A different search, a different reason. You are not buying water here — you are buying a golf-cart town and a club. So this section leaves the piers and the depth soundings out and looks at the two things that decide it: whether the cart life is real, and what the amenities actually cost to carry.

The golf-cart part is real, and it is written down
Cape Charles permits golf carts on town streets where the limit is 25 mph or lower. Not on faster roads or state highways, except to cross at designated intersections. The driver must be 16 or older with a valid licence, the cart needs headlights, tail lights, turn signals, mirrors, a horn and a windshield, and any child under eight needs a DOT child restraint. The police have been running an education campaign and putting a sticker listing the ordinances on carts they inspect — which tells you both that it is genuinely allowed and that it is genuinely enforced.

Bay Creek is connected, not adjacent: the community publishes a Cart Path to Cape Charles map, and the HOA committed to an improved path from the main entrance along the north side of Cassatt Parkway to Old Cape Charles Road near the Rosenwald School. Residents ride in. The town itself is six blocks by six, which is exactly cart scale.
The amenities — with one correction
Private two-mile beach. Pool, and the clubhouse is the Coach House Tavern. A fitness centre they call the Life Center. Tennis and pickleball. 350 acres of preserve with nature trails. Gated entry. A marina.

The golf is 27 holes, not two courses. The Palmer 18 opened in 2001 and the Nicklaus 18 followed around 2006, but the Nicklaus course was cut to nine holes in 2020 when new owners took over. Every Bay Creek lot on the dashboard said "2 courses — Palmer & Nicklaus"; that was wrong and is now fixed on all eight. It is still a lot of golf. It is not what the board was claiming.
The money, and this is the part that changes the decision
The POA assessment is $2,076–$2,196 a year, confirmed off MLS on several lots. That part was never in doubt. The club is, and it turns out to matter more.

Bay Creek's own Membership Plan says each initial and resale purchaser of a residence or homesite in the "New Community" must acquire and maintain at least a Sports Membership, submit a membership agreement, and pay the deposit or initiation fee on or before closing. An eligibility fee can sit on top of the joining fee. That is mandatory, and it explicitly reaches vacant lots, not just houses.

Several listings on the board say the opposite — "optional membership for golf course and Life Center", and on one Bridgeton Drive lot an MLS note reading "not grandfathered into golf course dues". Both cannot be true of the same parcel. The likely reconciliation is the phrase "New Community": older sections may sit outside it, and the golf tier may be the genuinely optional part on top of a compulsory Sports membership. That is lot-specific and it is the single most important thing to settle before any offer.

On the figures the board carries — a $15,000 joining fee and about $197 a month — the real carry is roughly $4,400 to $4,500 a year plus a five-figure entry. Over a three-year hold before you build a thing, that is about $28,000. On the cheapest lot below, $89,500, the annual carry alone is around five per cent of the purchase price, every year, for a field.
Which reframes the ranking completely
The dashboard scores water access at 20 points and amenities at zero, because you took amenities out of the rubric back in run 7. For Bay Creek that is now exactly backwards: if the value here is carts and the club, then paying up for creek frontage is paying for the thing you do not want.

So on your own stated terms the pick is Lot 79 Charlestowne Dr — $89,500, pond-front, 0.211 acre, POA $2,076. It is the cheapest way through the gate, and its listing is one of the ones calling membership optional. Compare that with Lot 12 Waters Edge Dr at $199,900, which scores a point higher only because it has genuine tidal Kings Creek frontage — $110,000 more for water you have just said you do not need. The other cheap ways in are 18 Bridgeton at $105,000, Lot 111 Colony Dr at $110,000 (a sports membership is bundled, though whether the recurring dues are waived is unconfirmed), 20 Bridgeton at $125,000 and 19 Bridgeton at $129,900, which has been sitting about 102 days and is listed below tax assessment.
And the cheap alternative nobody mentions
10C Bay Dunes Chase, The Dunes at Bay Ridge — $189,500, 0.7 acre, about 420 feet from a Bay beach boardwalk. It is not Bay Creek: the amenities are a pool and tennis, and that is it. But the assessment is $720 a year against Bay Creek's $2,100 plus club, so it carries for roughly a sixth of the cost. If it turns out the Bay Creek membership really is compulsory and the number lands where the Membership Plan implies, this is the lot that makes you do the arithmetic twice.
Three calls, in this order
1. The POA, for whether this parcel is in the "New Community" and whether a Sports membership is compulsory on a vacant lot. 2. The club, for the current joining fee, eligibility fee and monthly dues — the Membership Plan and the Schedule of Dues, Fees and Charges are both public PDFs on baycreeklife.com, which I could not reach from here, so read them yourself. 3. Eva Noonan, 757-615-8700, on the Colony Drive lot specifically, for whether the bundled membership includes the recurring dues or only the joining fee.

One thing not to skip even though this section is about amenities: Northampton County requires a sealed flood elevation certificate, Cape Charles sits around three feet with most developed land at five to ten, and several of these lots have an AE zone with a five-foot base flood elevation within about 150 feet. That is a construction-cost question, not a view question, and it does not care what the amenities are like.

Before you go

  • Six hours of driving is the real number, and it is more than the Reedville version, not less. Montross is simply far. If that is too much, drop Matthews Cove and the day falls to about four and a half hours — but then it has one house on it.
  • Both houses are drive‑bys. Saturday showings need an appointment; nothing on this card does, except the call about whether Matthews Cove is still for sale.
  • All three Montross pins are street-level, not house-level. Matthews Cove Dr, Edgewater Ln and Ships Row all failed to resolve to a house number in the geocoder I checked; the street matched for the first two and only the creek for Cabin Point. Look at those pins before you trust them.
  • Drive times are routed door to door — 6h03 over 226 miles — with no allowance for traffic, which is where the twenty minutes of slack goes.
  • Bay Creek is not part of Saturday. It is two and a quarter hours the other way, on the Eastern Shore. That section is there to be read, not driven.

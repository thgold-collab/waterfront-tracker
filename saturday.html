What changed, and what it costs

Dropping the Tappahannock pair and the White Stone house took out everything that was only near water — a shared neighbourhood dock, a condo, and a "waterfront" that turned out to be a small unnamed lake. That was three of the four houses, so the day needed a new far end.

It is Reedville, at the very tip of the Neck on the Little Wicomico, and it is the only cluster left where the docks are real. Four houses there sit within fifteen minutes of each other, including the only house on the whole board with a stated depth at the end of its pier. The price is the drive: 5h35 instead of 4h35, and a two-hour run home from the tip. That is what buys the half-six start.

    Urbanna — and coffee

    Middlesex County · a colonial tobacco port since the 1680s · fifty-five minutes

  4. 9:25 stretch your legs

  9. 12:10 walk it

    Reedville — and lunch out of the boot

    Main Street historic district · thirty minutes

    The furthest point of the day and the best place on it to eat what you brought. Walk Main Street for the Victorians built on menhaden money — at the turn of the last century this was said to be among the wealthiest towns per head in the country, and the street still shows it, with the water at the end of it.

Internet, town by town

The counterintuitive bit: the fiber went to the countryside, not the towns
The Northern Neck's regional fibre-to-the-home network was declared complete in January 2025 — more than 10,000 locations across Lancaster, Northumberland, Richmond, Westmoreland and King George. But it was built for addresses "previously unable to access broadband", which means the rural ones. The town centres already had cable, so they were never in it.

So a town-boundary fibre percentage understates what a house down a creek road may have, and the three Reedville houses on this card sit exactly in the territory that project was built to serve. That is inference from the project's stated scope, not a check of these four addresses — which is why there is a link to do that below.

What that actually means for you
Warsaw is the only town where fibre is the likely answer rather than the lucky one, and it is the one town on this route you had no interest in. Everywhere else, the in-town answer is Breezeline cable: gigabit down, but cable upload is a fraction of that, and if "fast" includes video calls and sending files then upload is the number that will annoy you. Fibre is symmetrical; cable is not.

Tappahannock is mid-build. Essex County's own updates said areas south of town were live and the majority of the rest would be online by the end of May 2026, so it should be finished now — worth one call to confirm rather than assuming.

Do not count on it arriving. Virginia's remaining 133,000 unserved locations are funded under BEAD, but construction was slated to begin in autumn 2026. If an address has nothing today, "it's coming" is a multi-year answer. Buy the connection, not the promise.

Backstops, in order: Starlink reaches all of it. Verizon and T‑Mobile 5G home internet run about $35–50 a month and are genuinely good where the signal is, but signal at the end of a creek is exactly what you cannot assume — check it on your own phone while you are standing there Saturday. Before you go

  • Half six is not padding. Reedville is a two-hour run home from the tip, so the late end of the day is fixed and the only place to find time is the morning. Coffee opens at six, which is the one thing that makes the early start pleasant rather than grim.
  • Every house here is a drive-by, deliberately. Saturday showings need an appointment; the questions on this card do not. If one survives the kerb, that is when you ring.
  • The four houses cluster oddly. One at 1h45 and three at 2h00, all in the last third of the day. If the morning overruns, the thing to cut is Kilmarnock's fifteen minutes, not a house — you will be back in Kilmarnock.
  • Drive times are routed door to door, not estimated — 5h35 over 208 miles. No allowance for traffic, which is where the half hour of slack goes.
  • Three pins are street-level, not house-level. Forest Green Rd, Swann Ct and Pine Reach Dr resolved to the street but not the number in the geocoder I checked against; Fairport Rd and 504 Main St matched exactly. Glance at those three when Maps opens them.

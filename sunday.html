One free Sunday · two ways to spend it

Two Sundays

Same budget, same brief, completely different days. One is colonial river towns and the widest water in Virginia. The other is the open Chesapeake — a lighthouse, two wild beaches, and the only house on the board with a covered boathouse.

Option A · 4h driving
The Rappahannock

Two colonial ports, a two-mile bridge, and the mouth of the river where it meets the Bay.

Best if you want pretty towns you could picture living in, and a shorter day.
Option B · 4h45 driving
Gloucester & Mathews

An 1805 lighthouse across the marsh, a peat beach full of pine stumps, and Yorktown on the way home.

Best if you want wilder scenery and the more interesting of the two houses.

  3. 10:50

    Urbanna

    Middlesex County · a colonial port since the 1680s

    One of Virginia's original tobacco ports, small enough to walk end to end in half an hour. Cross Street down to the harbour on Urbanna Creek. Whether you would happily spend a Saturday here is a question only the sidewalk answers.

  4. 11:45

    Lunch — Virginia Street Cafe

    Urbanna · walkable from the harbour

    Scratch cooking with a real menu either side of the seafood — sandwiches, salads, chicken, steak — and listed as serving lunch seven days a week, which not much out here does.

    Phone first. Two Urbanna places show as closed as of mid‑2026: Cross Street Coffee & Cafe, and Portside Grill at 25 Cross St, which may have reopened as The Dockside at Port Urbanna. Marshall's Drug Store at 50 Cross St is the old lunch-counter fallback.
  5. 1:00

    The Norris Bridge

    VA‑3 · nearly two miles across

    The crossing is the event. You come up over the span and the Rappahannock opens out underneath you at close to its widest — the single best look at the river all day, and the moment that explains why people move out here.

  6. 1:20

    Windmill Point

    Westland Beach & public fishing pier · end of Windmill Point Rd

    The mouth of the river, where the Rappahannock gives out into the Chesapeake. A recently rebuilt county park with restored beach and a long accessible fishing pier — public, free, and about as much open water as Virginia offers in one view. An hour here is easy.

  8. 3:30

    Irvington

    Lancaster County · Carter's Creek

    The prettiest village on the Northern Neck and the right place to end. Walk the lanes and the Tides Inn grounds down to Carter's Creek. If Urbanna is the working port, this is the postcard — and the contrast is genuinely useful for deciding which kind of place you want.

  9. 4:30

    Belle Isle State Park — optional

    Roughly on the way home, off VA‑3

    892 acres of tidewater marsh peninsula with seven miles of Rappahannock shoreline, a boardwalk and a fishing pier. Worth it if nobody has had enough water yet; skip it without guilt.

  6. 2:15

    New Point Comfort

    Nature Conservancy preserve · dawn to dusk, free

    An accessible boardwalk out over the salt marsh to an observation deck, looking across to the New Point Comfort lighthouse of 1805 — now stranded offshore on its own scrap of island as the point eroded away around it. 105 acres on the Atlantic Flyway; nesting least terns in season.

    This is the single most striking thing on either day.

  7. 3:15

    Bethel Beach

    Natural Area Preserve · Mathews County

    A wild Chesapeake beach where the sand is laid over peat, so the shore is studded with the stumps of a drowned loblolly pine forest. Piping plover and the threatened northeastern beach tiger beetle nest here. Nothing manicured about it — which is the appeal.

  8. 4:30

    Yorktown — optional

    On the way home over the Coleman Bridge

    You cross the York on the Coleman swing bridge anyway, so the riverwalk, the beach and the battlefield are right there. Shops and waterfront tables if anyone wants a drink before the last hour of driving. Cut it if the day has run long.

  9. ~6:45

    Home on I‑64 West

Ask at every dock, either day

Dock and boating is 22 of the 100 points, and three of the four houses have no depth figure at all. These are the questions that move a score.

One piece of timing, whichever you pick

Pull a tide table before you leave and try to land at least one dock near low water. At high tide every pier looks fine. Option A's afternoon stop at Corrotoman is the flexible one — do Irvington first if low water falls late. On Option B, Holly Trail already has a stated depth, so aim the tide at Brush Neck.

Before you go

  • Every house is a drive‑by unless you call ahead. Sunday showings out here need an appointment — and that call is also how you get the depth figures, so it is worth making either way.
  • Corrotoman By The Bay is a subdivision with likely private roads. You may only see the neighbourhood and the water approach.
  • Phone the lunch stop. Rural Sunday hours change without notice, and two Urbanna places have already closed since the listings were written.
  • Drive times are estimates carried from the tracker, not independently measured.
